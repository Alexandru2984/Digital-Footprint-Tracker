How it works

An OSINT tool that lists every account with the same name is easy to build and hard to trust. This one keeps track of why each finding is connected to the target, and how strongly.

Run a free scanHow it works

1. From one input to several

An email address is also searched by its local part as a username, a username with separators by its variants, and an address on a domain of its own by that domain. Each derived identifier remembers how it was reached.

2. Following the leads

Findings name new identifiers: an address on a GitHub key, a domain proven on Keybase, a website on a profile, a phone number on a homepage. Up to two rounds of these leads are scanned, but only from accounts already likely to be the target’s, and never handles under shared platforms such as name.bsky.social.

4. Attribution

Every identifier gets the score of its strongest chain back to the input — the product of the links along it. An account two verified links away scores 96%; one that only shares a name scores about 40%. Names, places and organisations are weighed by these scores, so a stranger who shares a username cannot take over the profile.

5. The dossier

The report opens with the profile at a glance: the likely name, accounts confirmed (80% and up), probable (50–79%) and unconfirmed, the target’s websites and domains, contact details, and what has leaked.

6. Risk

The risk score adds up categories — breaches, exposed services, linked identities, contact details, accounts — each saturating so that volume alone cannot max it out. A breach report counts for what it holds: how many breaches, whether passwords, payment or identity data leaked, and how recent. Clean verdicts add nothing.

Privacy

Scan inputs and results are encrypted at rest, anonymous scans expire after 30 days, and there are no third-party scripts or analytics on this site. See the privacy notice.
