Domain OSINT

Everything public about a domain in one scan: who registered it, what runs on it, what it exposes and which services and accounts its owner uses.

Ownership

The registry’s RDAP record gives the registrar and the registration, update and expiry dates. For thin registries such as .com, the registrar’s own RDAP server is asked as well, where the registrant lives — and where it is not redacted, the name, organisation, email, phone and country are extracted. The registrant’s email and phone then become leads of their own.

A site also gives its owner away through the analytics and advertising accounts it embeds: a Google Analytics, Tag Manager or AdSense ID, or a Meta Pixel. The same ID on another site is the same owner’s — AdSense pays one publisher. The scanner reads them from the homepage and asks HackerTarget which other sites embed them; an ID on dozens of sites belongs to an agency or a platform and ties nothing.

Infrastructure and services

  • DNS: addresses, mail servers and TXT records.
  • Services in use: ownership tokens in TXT records, SPF senders and MX hosts reveal Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Atlassian, Stripe, Slack, Zoom, OpenAI and dozens more.
  • Subdomains from Certificate Transparency logs and passive DNS.
  • DNS history: the addresses the domain pointed to over the years, from passive DNS. An address it used before moving behind a CDN may still be its origin server.
  • Every IP the domain resolves to is checked for open ports, CVEs and its network operator. A CDN’s edge (Cloudflare, Fastly…) is named as such instead of being reported as the domain’s own open ports.

Exposure

  • Exposed files: .env files, .git directories, database dumps and backups, confirmed by their content, not just a 200.
  • Web posture: TLS and HTTP security headers, graded.
  • Mail security: whether mail from the domain can be spoofed (SPF, DMARC, DKIM, MTA-STS).
  • Lookalike domains that resolve — candidates for phishing.
  • Subdomain takeover: subdomains whose CNAME points at GitHub Pages, S3, Heroku, Azure and other services where nothing is claimed any more — anyone could claim the name and serve content on the subdomain.

The people behind it

The homepage is read for the accounts its owner marks with rel=me, the profiles it links, a contact address and phone. Keybase and Bluesky are asked who proved control of the domain. The Wayback Machine shows how long it has existed.

Questions

Can I see who owns a domain?

When the registration is not redacted, yes — many ccTLDs, organisations and older registrations still name the holder. Otherwise the homepage, Keybase and Bluesky proofs often identify the owner.

How are subdomains found?

From Certificate Transparency logs, which record every publicly trusted certificate, and from historical DNS.

Is the scan intrusive?

No. It reads public records and fetches a handful of well-known paths, as a browser would.

How is a subdomain takeover detected?

From DNS alone, or one page load: the subdomain’s CNAME target no longer exists, or the service it points at answers with its “nothing here” page. Nothing is claimed or registered.
