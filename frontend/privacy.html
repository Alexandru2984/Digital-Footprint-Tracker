Privacy Notice

Last updated: 30 August 2026

This notice explains what personal data this service handles and why. It covers two different groups of people, because an OSINT tool necessarily processes information about people who never signed up for it:

  • Account holders and visitors — people who use the scanner.
  • Scan subjects — people whose email address, username, domain, IP address or phone number is submitted as a scan target by someone else.

1. Who is responsible

2. What the service does

You submit one identifier. The service queries public sources and third-party APIs for traces of that identifier, then stores the findings so you can review, export and monitor them. It does not attempt to access private accounts, bypass authentication, or retrieve anything that is not already publicly reachable.

3. Data about account holders and visitors

  • Account data: username, email address, a hashed password (never the password itself), and — if you enable it — two-factor authentication secrets and notification destinations (webhook, Discord, Slack or Telegram).
  • Usage data: your scans, their results, saved investigations, tags and exports.
  • Technical logs: an audit trail of security-relevant actions. IP addresses are truncated to a network prefix (/24 for IPv4, /48 for IPv6) before being recorded, and the truncated value is stored encrypted. Application logs record a coarse target kind such as “email” or “non_email”, never the target itself.

Scanning is available without an account. Anonymous scans are not linked to an identity, and are deleted automatically after 30 days.

4. Data about scan subjects

When someone scans an identifier, this service stores that identifier and the findings returned for it. If the identifier belongs to a person, that is personal data about them, processed without their involvement.

Safeguards applied to it:

  • Scan inputs, results, result metadata and exports are encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM using per-field key separation.
  • Findings are visible only to the account that ran the scan (or, for an anonymous scan, to whoever holds the scan link) — never publicly listed or indexed.
  • Retention is bounded by default (see section 7).
  • The service records only what the queried sources already publish; it adds no inference about health, beliefs, sexuality, or any other special category of data.

If you are the subject of a scan and want to know whether your data is held here, or want it erased, write to the privacy contact in section 1. See section 8 for what we can and cannot do.

5. Legal basis

  • Account data and delivering the service: performance of a contract (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)).
  • Scan subject data: legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)) in letting people assess exposure of identifiers they own or are authorised to assess, and in security research. This basis is only valid where the person running the scan has a lawful reason to do so — which is why the Acceptable Use Policy makes that their responsibility and a condition of using the service.
  • Security logging and abuse prevention: legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)).

6. Who the data is shared with

To answer a scan, the submitted identifier is sent to the public sources relevant to its type. These are independent controllers with their own privacy policies. Depending on the identifier, they may include:

  • Breach and reputation: Have I Been Pwned, VirusTotal, AbuseIPDB, Shodan / InternetDB.
  • Account presence: GitHub, GitLab, Reddit, Keybase, Mastodon, Telegram, Steam, npm, PyPI, Hacker News, Gravatar, X/Twitter, Pastebin, and the public platform list used by the bulk username sweep.
  • Infrastructure: crt.sh, RDAP/WHOIS, the Wayback Machine, HackerTarget passive DNS, and public DNS-over-HTTPS resolvers.
  • Phone validation: AbstractAPI (only for phone-shaped input, and only if the operator has configured a key).

Several of these are outside the EU/EEA, so a scan may involve an international transfer. Sending an identifier to them is inherent to what a scan is.

Separately, the service uses Resend to send transactional and alert email, and Cloudflare as a CDN and protective proxy in front of the site. IP geolocation is resolved locally from an offline database, so scanned IP addresses are not sent to a geolocation provider.

Data is not sold, and not used for advertising or profiling.

7. How long it is kept

  • Anonymous scans: deleted automatically 30 days after creation.
  • Account scans: deleted automatically according to the retention period set on your account — 30 days by default. You may shorten it, or set “never”, in which case your scans persist until you delete them or your account.
  • Account data: kept while the account exists.
  • Audit records: retained for security accountability in a tamper-evident, append-only ledger. Because that ledger is integrity-chained, entries are redacted rather than removed.
  • Backups: encrypted daily snapshots are retained on a rolling basis, so deleted data may persist in a backup for a short period before ageing out.

8. Your rights

Under the GDPR you may request access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, and object to processing based on legitimate interests.

  • If you have an account: you can export your data and delete your account yourself from account settings. Deleting the account removes your scans, results and saved investigations.
  • If you are a scan subject: write to the privacy contact. Please include the identifier concerned. Note the limits honestly: this service holds a copy of findings, it is not the source. Erasing a record here does not remove anything from GitHub, Have I Been Pwned, the Wayback Machine or any other original source — those requests have to go to them.

You also have the right to complain to a supervisory authority. In Romania that is the National Supervisory Authority for Personal Data Processing (ANSPDCP).

9. Security

Sensitive fields are encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM under separated, versioned keys. Traffic is served over TLS. The application runs as a dedicated unprivileged system identity from an immutable, commit-pinned release, with its secrets supplied as encrypted systemd credentials rather than environment variables. Backups are encrypted and their restorability is rehearsed. Security events are written to a signed, append-only ledger.

No system is immune. If you find a vulnerability, see security.txt.
