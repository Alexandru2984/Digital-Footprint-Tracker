Data breach check

See which breaches an email address has leaked in, what each one exposed and how serious it is.

Run a free scanHow it works

What you get

  • Every breach the address appears in, newest first, with the year and how many accounts it exposed.
  • What leaked: passwords, card details, identity documents, phone numbers, addresses.
  • Weak password storage: breaches where passwords were kept in plain text or in an easily cracked form.
  • A risk score that weighs breaches by what they hold, not by how many there are: a recent leak of passwords and card numbers counts far more than an old mailing list.

Breach data comes from XposedOrNot and LeakCheck, both free and without a key, and optionally from Have I Been Pwned. The indexes overlap, so a breach both list is counted once, and the risk score weighs an address’s fullest report rather than adding up the copies.

What to do next

  1. Change the password of every account that shared a leaked password, starting with email and banking.
  2. Turn on two-factor authentication wherever it is offered.
  3. Expect targeted phishing that quotes leaked details, and verify unexpected messages through another channel.
  4. If card or identity data leaked, watch statements and consider a credit freeze.

Questions

Is the breach check free?

Yes. It needs no account and no key.

Is my email stored?

Scans are encrypted at rest and anonymous scans are deleted after 30 days. See the privacy notice.

Why does my score rise with breaches that leaked passwords?

Because a leaked password is directly usable against you, while a leaked address mostly means more spam.
