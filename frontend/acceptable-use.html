Acceptable Use Policy

Last updated: 30 August 2026

This tool aggregates public traces of an identifier. That is useful for understanding your own exposure, and it is equally useful for stalking someone. The difference is not in the tool — it is in who you point it at and why. This policy is the line, and accepting it is a condition of using the service.

1. The rule

Scan only identifiers you own, or that you are lawfully authorised to assess. If you cannot explain, to a regulator, why you had a lawful basis to look up a particular person, do not run the scan.

2. Permitted

  • Your own email addresses, usernames, phone numbers, domains and IP addresses.
  • Identifiers belonging to an organisation you work for or act for, where you are authorised to assess its exposure.
  • Domains and infrastructure you administer, or are engaged to test under a written authorisation.
  • Security research and incident response conducted within the law and, for personal data, on a valid legal basis.

3. Prohibited

You must not use this service to:

  • Stalk, harass, intimidate, threaten or surveil any person.
  • Compile or publish a dossier on a private individual (“doxxing”), or expose someone's identity, location, employer or associations against their interests.
  • Target a person you have no lawful basis to investigate — including ex-partners, family members, neighbours, colleagues, journalists, activists, or anyone who has asked to be left alone.
  • Support discrimination, or make decisions about employment, credit, housing, insurance or immigration on the basis of findings here.
  • Enable credential stuffing, account takeover, phishing, social engineering, or any attempt to access an account or system.
  • Circumvent rate limits, quotas or authentication — including by scripting the anonymous endpoint or spreading load across accounts or addresses.
  • Bulk-enumerate identifiers to build a mailing, marketing or resale list.
  • Resell, sublicense or redistribute results as a commercial data product.
  • Breach the terms of any upstream source the service queries, or the law of any jurisdiction that applies to you or to your target.

4. Results are evidence of a trace, not of a person

A finding means an identifier matched something a public source published. It does not prove that a particular human being controls that account, and the service says so explicitly: derived and inferred findings carry a reduced confidence score and a provenance marker. Treat confidence scores as a starting point for verification, never as a conclusion — and never act against a person on an unverified match.

Upstream sources can be stale, wrong, or deliberately poisoned. The service reports what they said, at the time they said it.

5. Third-party terms

Findings come from independent providers, each with its own terms and licensing. Where a provider restricts redistribution or commercial use, those restrictions travel with the data you receive here.

6. Enforcement

The service applies rate limits to every caller, and reserves heavy, high-fan-out scanning for verified accounts. Where a violation of this policy is identified, the operator may throttle or suspend the account, delete the offending scans, and, where legally required, preserve records and notify the relevant authority.
